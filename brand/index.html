The idea

A table with lovely objects on it. That’s our shop, and that’s our logo.

Studio table is a second-hand shop in Stockholm.  Odd, lovely, used and curated. Our shop is filled with epic antiques and kitchenware. Our brand is human like the objects we put in it.

Our logo "bord" is the table that holds our lovely things. Hand drawn and imperfect to mirror ourselves and our brand. Like our shop, that changes from day to day,  the logo holds a bunch of trinkets and things. Everything used by someone else, looking to be placed on another table.

The responsive shelf

The signature move: the table is constant, the wares scale with the space. Strip back to a bare table at favicon size; load the full shelf at hero size.

Icon · 16–48pxBare table
Compact1 ware
Medium3 wares
Hero6 wares

Rule of thumb: add one ware for every ~120px of additional width, and use the smallest size that still reads — there’s no single default. Below 48px, drop the wares entirely and let the table carry the brand.

Misuse

The system is loose by design. A few rules keep it on track.

Don’t recolourBlue & beige only — never a third colour.
Don’t distortKeep the table’s proportions.
Don’t place off-paletteNo foreign background colours.
STUDIO TABLE
Don’t substitute the fontCrimson Pro only.
Don’t over-straightenKeep the hand-drawn wobble.
Don’t alter the tableNever add overhangs, extra legs, or change the silhouette — the table is fixed.
Don’t vary the thicknessThe top bar and legs always share one consistent weight — never thicken or thin one against the other.

Colour

Two families, nothing else. An electric blue that feels like fresh ink, and a warm beige like aged paper. The contrast is the whole personality.

Ink Blue on Paper clears WCAG AA for text. Deep Blue carries large fills, hovers, and shadow; Sand handles panels, borders, and quiet surfaces. The whole system runs on blue and beige.

The wares

A drawn library of objects that live on the table. Use them on the logo, on their own as icons, or scattered as a motif. The set is open — new finds become new drawings.

Drawn in the same hand as the table: single-weight line, rounded ends, the same wobble. Treat them as a growing collection — a candlestick today, a typewriter tomorrow.

Where next

How to make the brand fuller and more scalable — a shortlist to take into motion and web.

01 · MOTION

The shelf assembles.

Animate the logo as a hand drawing the table, then wares “placed” one by one. The stroke wobble is already vector — perfect for draw-on and a gentle idle sway.

02 · WEBSITE

A shelf you scroll.

Treat the catalogue as an endless table: products sit on drawn shelves, the header logo gains/sheds wares with viewport width using the responsive rule.

03 · ICON LIBRARY

Grow the wares.

Keep drawing objects as new stock arrives — a named, versioned set. They double as category icons, stickers, and packing-tape print.

04 · PHOTOGRAPHY

One blue prop.

Shoot real pieces on beige with a single blue element in frame (a painted wall, a cloth) so photos lock to the palette without filters.

05 · VOICE

Dry & fond.

Short, honest object descriptions with a wink — “Chipped, charming, cheaper for it.” Build a copy bank so the tone scales past one writer.

06 · TYPE SCALE

Lock the tokens.

Promote the sizes on this page to named tokens (display / heading / body / label) so web, print, and signage stay in step.

Assets

The core marks, ready to use — blue on transparent. SVG scales cleanly for print and signage; PNG suits slides and the web.
